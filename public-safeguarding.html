Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy

Purpose and scope

Fenland Angels Radio (FAR) commits to protecting children (people under 18) participating in programmes, training, volunteering, online activity and community events. This policy applies to staff, volunteers, presenters, DJs, managers, contractors and guests working with young people.

Principles

The welfare, dignity, voice and safety of the young person come first. FAR will treat concerns seriously; act promptly; avoid discrimination; make reasonable adjustments; and never promise secrecy about harm. Young people can stop participating or raise a concern without retaliation.

Leadership and contacts

Designated Safeguarding Lead and accountable station lead: Nathaniel Daw, Founder and Station Manager. Contact fenlandangelsradio@gmail.com with the subject Safeguarding. The inbox is not an emergency service. Do not send detailed sensitive evidence in your first message; ask for a confidential contact route. If the concern involves Nathaniel, reporting internally is unsafe, or he cannot be reached, contact the child's local council children's social care service or the police directly. No alternative station officer is named until their appointment and training are completed.

Pre-participation safeguards

Before a person under 18 takes part regularly: assess the activity and setting; obtain FAR’s written parent/guardian participation permission and the young person’s agreement; appoint a supervisor; agree broadcast location and time; complete induction and emergency contacts; review suitable recruitment and DBS eligibility for each adult role; and authorise first broadcast in writing.

Responding to concerns

Listen without leading questions, reassure without promising confidentiality, record factual details promptly, and inform the DSL. If the concern involves the DSL or reporting internally is unsafe, contact the relevant local authority children’s social care team or police directly. Call 999 for immediate danger. Do not investigate or confront the alleged person.

Allegations about adults

Preserve information, restrict access, take immediate protective steps proportionately, and seek advice from the relevant local authority designated officer (LADO) where the allegations threshold may be met. FAR must not investigate first or make promises about outcomes. Where required, consider referrals to DBS and other authorities with professional advice.

Review and accountability

The DSL maintains a confidential incident log, training and policy register and reviews arrangements at least annually and after significant concerns. Safeguarding records are kept separately from ordinary staff records with restricted access and an approved retention schedule.

Training and readiness

The lead and adults supervising young people must complete appropriate role-specific safeguarding training before supervising an under-18 activity. Management must record the risk assessment, recruitment checks, adult supervision and permission arrangements before the first show. This policy does not certify that an individual is trained or DBS-cleared.

Staff and Volunteer Code of Conduct

Expected behaviour

Treat young people respectfully; use professional language; maintain clear boundaries; listen to concerns; avoid favouritism, bullying, discriminatory remarks, gifts or promises that create obligations; and follow FAR safeguarding instructions.

Contact and meetings

Use approved FAR channels. Avoid one-to-one private messaging, disappearing messages and personal social media connections. For training or meetings use visible/recorded arrangements where appropriate, agreed times, and parent/guardian awareness; do not conduct unapproved home visits or transport young people alone.

Conduct prohibited

No sexualised comments or relationships with a young person, intimidation, humiliation, inappropriate physical contact, substance use during supervision, or sharing private details. Do not seek secrecy from parents or management. Report breaches promptly.

Reporting

Any safeguarding concern goes to the DSL or alternative/external route. Staff must not investigate themselves. Breaches may lead to restricted access, suspension of duties and referral to relevant agencies.

Online Safety and Listener Contact

Approved communications

Young presenters use station-managed email, moderated comments and approved team channels only. Adult staff must not use personal direct messages to arrange activities with a young presenter. Record scheduling and important decisions in a shared authorised channel; copy in a parent/guardian where appropriate.

On-air privacy

Do not reveal a young presenter’s home address, school/college timetable, travel routes, personal phone, personal social accounts or live location. Public stage names require agreement. Delay posting location-sensitive images until safe.

Live calls and messages

No unscreened live callers, open phone lines or unmoderated listener messages involving under-18 presenters. An authorised adult moderates incoming contact and can disconnect or end the segment. Save and report abusive, grooming or threatening contact using restricted evidence procedures.

Account access

Use individual least-privilege accounts, strong authentication, and no shared master passwords. The adult station operator controls live source routing and emergency takeover. Revoke access promptly when a placement ends.

Recording, Photography and Publicity

Programme recording

FAR may record programmes for operational quality, complaints handling and agreed programme use where it has identified and documented a lawful basis. The presenter and parent/guardian must receive a clear explanation of recordings and retention.

Optional publicity

Separate optional permission is requested for promotional photographs, video clips, named biographies, social media posts and third-party publicity. Refusal of optional publicity does not automatically prevent participation. Obtain the young person’s views; avoid revealing personal identifiers.

Withdrawal and limitations

A request to stop future optional publicity will be respected where practicable. Previously broadcast or shared content may not be fully retractable; explain this before publication. Safeguarding disclosures may be shared without consent where legally justified.

Complaints, Raising Concerns and Whistleblowing

How to raise a concern

Young people, parents, listeners and staff can report a concern to Nathaniel Daw at fenlandangelsradio@gmail.com. If the concern involves him, or reporting internally is unsafe, use the council children's social care or police routes in the contact section. Ordinary programme complaints can be sent to the same station email with the subject Complaint.

What FAR will do

Acknowledge when safe, make a factual record, assess urgency, protect the young person, consult relevant agencies and provide appropriate updates without sharing confidential information. No retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith.

Emergency

Immediate danger: call 999. Non-emergency police: 101. Contact the child's council children's social care team directly using the reporting routes at the end of this pack. FAR must not substitute its own investigation for statutory agencies.

Young People’s Privacy Notice — Supplement

Who handles your information

Bastone Major Group Ltd, operating Fenland Angels Radio, is responsible for personal information handled for FAR activities. Contact Nathaniel Daw at fenlandangelsradio@gmail.com for privacy enquiries or requests. This notice explains information used for under-18 participation; it supplements the station's general privacy information.

What we need and why

We may collect the young person's and guardian's names and contact details, age where needed, agreed participation times, permissions, relevant safety or accessibility needs, induction records, programme recordings and records of concerns. Information comes from the young person, guardian, staff or relevant agencies. We collect only information necessary for the activity or concern. Publicity is optional; necessary safety information must be available before participation can proceed.

Lawful bases

For routine coordination, safe programme operation and handling complaints, FAR relies on legitimate interests in running a safe radio service and must assess necessity, children's interests and reasonable expectations before processing. Optional publicity uses consent, with guardian involvement and the young person's views. Consent can be withdrawn for future use. Appropriate safeguarding processing may rely on recognised legitimate interests; a specific legal obligation applies only where an actual applicable duty exists. Vital interests may apply to necessary life-saving action.

Sensitive information

Health or other special-category information requires an additional lawful condition. Where appropriate, this can be explicit consent for agreed adjustments, or the safeguarding condition under UK law where its requirements are met. Criminal-offence or DBS information needs separate authority and safeguards; FAR must check eligibility and lawful handling before collecting it. Participation permission is not automatically consent to all data use.

Sharing and protection

Information may be shared with authorised FAR staff who need it, service providers acting for the station, guardians where appropriate, and police, social care or other appropriate safeguarding agencies when necessary and lawful. We must not promise secrecy where a child may be at risk. Safeguarding files must be separated from routine staff information, protected against unauthorised access and never uploaded to the public website. Any overseas service-provider transfer requires lawful safeguards and an assessment before use. FAR does not sell children's information or use it for automated eligibility decisions.

How long information is kept

Routine participation and permission records are reviewed when a placement ends and must be deleted when no longer needed for administration, consent evidence or a continuing complaint. Recordings used only for operational checks are deleted when those checks and any related complaint are complete; separately agreed published material follows its explained use and withdrawal arrangements. Safeguarding records require case-specific retention based on the child's age, seriousness, ongoing protection needs, agency advice and relevant legal requirements. The lead must record the retention decision, review at least annually and delete securely when the purpose expires. A live referral, dispute or lawful preservation requirement may prevent immediate deletion.

Your rights

You can ask for access, correction, deletion or restriction, and object to processing based on legitimate interests. Portability applies where its legal conditions are met. Rights depend on the circumstances and may be limited to protect another person or meet lawful obligations. Contact the station email; FAR normally responds within one month, with any permitted extension explained. You can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint or 0303 123 1113. Safeguarding information may be shared without consent where lawfully justified.

Young Presenter and Parent Information

Welcome

FAR gives young people a chance to learn radio and media skills in a supportive environment. You will have an agreed show plan, training, a named adult supervisor and a safe way to raise concerns.

Before your first show

We will agree where and when you broadcast, check equipment and online safety, explain what happens if something goes wrong, obtain written participation permissions and confirm the adult monitoring the programme.

Your choices and support

You can ask questions, say no to optional publicity, request reasonable adjustments, pause a show or report anything that worries you. You will not be expected to handle abusive listeners alone.

Contacts

Station safeguarding lead: Nathaniel Daw, fenlandangelsradio@gmail.com. For concerns about the lead, or if it is unsafe to contact FAR, use the external council or police reporting routes. Immediate danger: 999.
